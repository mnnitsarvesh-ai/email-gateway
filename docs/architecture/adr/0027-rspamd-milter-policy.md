# ADR-0027: Rspamd at SMTP time: what the inbound milter may do, and how it fails

**Status:** Accepted. Amends [ADR-0006](0006-postfix-mail-path.md) (the Rspamd milter no longer fails closed: `milter_default_action = accept`; the policy service still fails closed) and [ADR-0019](0019-smtp-gateway-topology.md) (inbound milter settings). Takes over the SMTP-time part of [ADR-0007](0007-security-engines.md). Design: [step 4.1](../../plan/steps/09-rspamd.md) §S11.

**Context.** The inbound Postfix (`infrastructure/postfix/main.cf`) already uses the Rspamd proxy worker in milter mode (`smtpd_milters = inet:rspamd:11332`, `self_scan = yes`) with `milter_default_action = tempfail`, Rspamd rejecting at score ≥ 15 and adding `X-Spamd-Result` and `Authentication-Results`. Step 4.1 adds the authoritative post-queue scan (ADR-0026): every accepted message is scanned again from S3 with its trusted envelope and the result is recorded; a failed scan is `scan_failed`, never clean. The question is what the SMTP-time pass may still decide, and what happens to the SMTP session when Rspamd is unavailable.

**Decision.**

1. **Mechanism unchanged:** Rspamd's proxy worker in milter mode on 11332 (`self_scan`), milter protocol 6, Postfix macros for client address/name, HELO, queue id, envelope sender and SASL user. One platform-wide behaviour: an SMTP session may carry recipients of several tenants, so SMTP-time behaviour is not per tenant (per-tenant thresholds apply post-queue only).
2. **Reject only on very high confidence; otherwise annotate.** The milter may answer `554 5.7.1` only for (a) the GTUBE test pattern and (b) a score at or above `RSPAMD_MILTER_REJECT_SCORE` (default 15). DMARC `p=reject` failures are **not** rejected at SMTP time by default (`RSPAMD_MILTER_DMARC_REJECT=false`): forwarders and mailing lists break DMARC, ARC is not universally deployed, and a post-queue `auth.dmarc_reject` reason lets policy quarantine instead of bouncing legitimate forwarded mail. Operators may switch it on platform-wide. No greylisting, no soft reject, no subject rewriting, no `add header` action at SMTP time.
3. **Headers.** Inbound headers that could forge our results (`X-Spamd-Result`, `X-Rspamd-*`, `X-Spam*`, and `Authentication-Results` carrying our authserv-id) are removed by **Postfix cleanup**, not only by the milter: `header_checks = regexp:/etc/postfix/header_checks`, rendered by the Postfix entrypoint from `POSTFIX_MYHOSTNAME` and `RSPAMD_AUTHSERV_ID` (`IGNORE` for an `Authentication-Results` whose authserv-id, after an optional comment, is ours; for `X-Spam*`; for `X-Rspamd-*`). This runs for every message before it is queued, whether or not the milter answers, so the protection does not depend on Rspamd being up (decision 4). `header_checks` do not apply to headers a milter adds (`milter_header_checks` is unset), so the milter still adds one `Authentication-Results` header with our authserv-id; its own removal routine (`eg-strip-forged`) stays as a second layer and finds nothing left to remove. No score headers are added (scores are not leaked to recipients or attackers; the recorded result is in MySQL). Downstream consumers of the EML (post-queue scan, archive, recipients) never trust any other `Authentication-Results`. (Review round 2 of step 4.1: before this, the milter was the only place that stripped them, so with Rspamd down a sender's forged `dmarc=pass` result with our authserv-id was stored and delivered; `tests/integration/scan-smtp.test.ts` now covers Rspamd down.)
4. **Fail open to accept.** `milter_default_action = accept`, `milter_connect_timeout = 10s`, `milter_content_timeout = 30s` (Rspamd proxy timeout below it). When Rspamd is down or slow, Postfix accepts the message without the SMTP-time pass (forged result headers are still removed by cleanup, decision 3, and the stored copy carries no `Authentication-Results` of ours); the message is stored and **always** scanned post-queue, where Rspamd unavailability yields `timeout`/`error` attempts, retries and finally `scan_failed`, never `clean`. The miss is visible (Postfix logs the default action; the scan stage's breaker and error metrics alert).

**Alternatives considered.**

- _`tempfail` (ADR-0006)_: rejected. The SMTP-time pass is no longer the security control of record, so failing closed would turn every Rspamd incident into an inbound mail outage (remote MTAs back off for minutes to hours and some give up), while the only protection lost by accepting is the early reject of GTUBE and score ≥ 15 mail (which the post-queue scan classifies anyway) and the `Authentication-Results` header of our own check. This holds only because the removal of forged headers does not depend on the milter (decision 3).
- _Strip forged headers in the milter only_: rejected in review round 2. With `accept` on failure the forged copy would be stored and delivered whenever Rspamd is down, restarting, overloaded or over its proxy timeout.
- _Headers only, never reject_: rejected. Rejecting near-certain spam in-session costs nothing (no backscatter, the sender's MTA owns the bounce) and saves storage and scanning.
- _Reject on DMARC `p=reject` by default_: rejected (see decision 2); available as configuration.
- _No SMTP-time milter at all (post-queue only)_: rejected. The milter is how mail is rejected in-session, and its `Authentication-Results` is useful to tenant mailbox providers.
- _Per-tenant SMTP-time thresholds through Rspamd settings by recipient_: rejected. Multi-tenant sessions make one recipient's tenant decide for another's.

**Consequences.** Inbound mail is scanned twice (milter and post-queue); Rspamd capacity is sized for both (production can use a separate SMTP-time Rspamd pool). During an Rspamd outage spam that would have been rejected is accepted and classified post-queue (or ends `scan_failed` and is held by policy). ADR-0006's "both fail closed" now applies to the policy service only.
