# ADR-0049: Policy copy body edits — WARN banners and URL_REWRITE after the scan; the store behind QUARANTINE

**Status:** Accepted (implemented in step 5.3, verified locally on 2026-11-12: WARN banners (`applied`, `skipped_signed`), URL rewriting after the scan with click-service resolution and release copies verified by the `quarantine-smtp` stack suite; [§12, §13](../../plan/steps/17-quarantine.md#13-verification-results); not verified: Kubernetes apply, CI). Design: [step 5.3](../../plan/steps/17-quarantine.md) Q22, Q23, Q13.6, Q2. **Supersedes** the `QUARANTINE`, `WARN` and `URL_REWRITE` rows and the "writing quarantine rows in 5.2" and "URL_REWRITE by asking the URL engine to rewrite after the decision" alternatives of [ADR-0045](0045-policy-action-semantics.md) (its other rows stay in force). **Amends** [ADR-0030](0030-url-rewriting-and-click-tokens.md) (token signing custody adds the policy-engine, sign-only with the current key) and the step 4.3 U20 delivered-object rule for releases.

**Context.** ADR-0045 recorded WARN body banners as `not_supported`, URL_REWRITE after the scan as recorded only (the round's rewritten copy or nothing), and QUARANTINE as "held, no store". Step 5.3 must make these real: a banner as a MIME body edit in the policy copy that is safe for signed or encrypted mail, and URL rewriting of the policy copy through the click service or an explicit decision to keep it a 4.3 setting. The 4.3 rewriter (`rewriteEml`) and token format are reusable; the click-service resolves tokens by message and URL sequence regardless of the signer.

**Decision.**

1. **QUARANTINE** creates per-recipient entries in the evaluation transaction (ADR-0046) — status `enforced`.
2. **WARN** adds the 5.2 headers/subject prefix **and** a body banner: the first primary `text/plain` and `text/html` parts of the policy copy get a fixed-template banner (rule text escaped), re-encoded with the original transfer encoding; untouched parts stay byte-identical. Signed (S/MIME, PGP) or encrypted messages, oversized messages and parts that cannot be decoded losslessly are passed through and recorded (`skipped_signed`, `skipped_encrypted`, `skipped_too_large`, `skipped_no_text_part`, `skipped_part`). Status `enforced` when applied, `recorded` otherwise.
3. **URL_REWRITE after the scan** is performed by the policy-engine with `rewriteEml` and tokens signed with `URL_TOKEN_KEY_CURRENT` when the round was not rewritten only because the tenant's 4.3 setting is off for the message (`disabled`, `direction`). Other skip reasons cannot be fixed; with `ifNotRewritten = quarantine` the engine re-evaluates with the policy rewrite's skip reason (deterministic) and the message is quarantined. The 4.3 tenant setting remains the default, scan-time path. A switch `POLICY_URL_REWRITE_ENABLED` can turn policy rewriting off (then URL_REWRITE is recorded only, as in 5.2).
4. **Copy pipeline order:** base object → URL rewrite → banner → header edits; one deterministic key per round (evaluation) or per release. A release reuses the re-validated scan round's rewritten copy while it exists, else the original, and always writes a fresh policy copy with `X-EG-Quarantine-Release`.

**Alternatives considered.** Keep URL rewriting a 4.3-only setting: rejected — a tenant rule demanding rewriting would silently do nothing for tenants with rewriting off. A second URL-engine pass requested by policy: rejected (another queue hop, scan latency on the delivery path). Banners as headers only: does not meet the brief. Banners in signed mail: rejected (breaks the signature the recipient relies on).

**Consequences.** The policy-engine holds the URL token signing key (no ingress, sign-only, rotated with the 4.3 procedure). Inbound DKIM body signatures no longer verify downstream when a banner or rewrite is applied (documented, as for 4.3). Evaluator version `policy-eval/2`: dry-runs of a round whose policy rewrite was skipped and re-evaluated report `reproducible: false` with that cause.
