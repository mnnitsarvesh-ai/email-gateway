# ADR-0026: Security scan pipeline: per-engine queues, scan rounds, engine registry, standardized verdicts

**Status:** Accepted (`url` engine registered and URL child rows added by [ADR-0029](0029-url-security-engine.md); engine dependencies — stage-2 engines triggered by `mail.message.scan_stage_ready.v1`, written once per round by the transaction that inserts the last dependency final — and the required `bec` engine added by [ADR-0033](0033-bec-engine-and-stage-dependencies.md), implemented in step 4.4: decisions 1 and 3 hold for stage-1 engines; the orchestrator gained one generic, catalog-driven capability and names no engine; the stage-1 `dlp` engine registered by [ADR-0036](0036-dlp-engine-and-extraction.md), implemented in step 4.5 with no orchestrator change beyond its child-row branch in the finalize transaction; the asynchronous, never-required `sandbox` engine added by [ADR-0040](0040-sandbox-engine-dispatch-results-artifacts.md), implemented in step 4.6: the one orchestrator rule — `aggregateRound` ignores finals of asynchronous engines, which are also excluded from `SCAN_ENGINES` and the round's required set — so a sandbox final, written before or after completion, never changes a round, [§13](../../plan/steps/14-sandbox.md#13-verification-results); risk scoring at completion and the profile snapshot at round open added by [ADR-0042](0042-risk-scoring-placement-and-policy-handoff.md), implemented in step 5.1: the completion transaction also writes the round's `message_risk_scores` revision 0 and `email_scans.verdict`, `risk_score`, `scoring_version` before the lifecycle transition). Supersedes [ADR-0007](0007-security-engines.md) (the engine contract and its outcome set are replaced; Rspamd, ClamAV and YARA remain the chosen engines; the SMTP-time part of ADR-0007 is now [ADR-0027](0027-rspamd-milter-policy.md)). Amends [ADR-0024](0024-rabbitmq-topology-retry-dead-letter.md) (topology version 3: `scan.spam` becomes a work queue, `scan.auth` loses its bindings, a `scanner` role user) and [ADR-0025](0025-message-lifecycle-state-machine.md) (edge E27 `scanned → approved` for the development pass-through; E4 `persisted → approved` retired). Design: [step 4.1](../../plan/steps/09-rspamd.md).

**Context.** Phase 4 adds six engines (Rspamd 4.1, ClamAV and YARA 4.2, URL protection 4.3, phishing/BEC 4.4, DLP 4.5, sandbox 4.6). Step 3.2 bound `mail.message.parsed.v1` and (3.3) `mail.message.rescan_requested.v1` to five dormant scan queues, step 3.3 defined the lifecycle states `scanning`, `scanned` and `scan_failed` with rounds, and `email_scans` / `scan_engine_results` exist since step 1.1 without a writer. ADR-0007 described one in-process fan-out per message and an outcome set (`clean | suspicious | malicious | error | timeout`) that does not contain the brief's `SPAM` and `UNKNOWN`. The brief requires standardized verdicts `CLEAN, SPAM, SUSPICIOUS, MALICIOUS, UNKNOWN, TIMEOUT, ERROR`, engine errors never classified as clean, per-tenant thresholds, engine version tracking, timeouts, circuit breakers, idempotent processing and rescans that keep old results.

**Decision.**

1. **Choreography over per-engine queues, not a central fan-out.** Each registered engine consumes its own scan queue (Rspamd: `scan.spam`) with its own consumer, retry budget, prefetch and breaker, so a slow or failed engine never holds the others' capacity. The first engine handler of a message opens the **scan round** (one `email_scans` row per `(message, lifecycle_round)`, unique) and moves the message `persisted → scanning` (E3) through the step 3.3 transition service; every engine records exactly one final result per round (`scan_engine_results`, unique per `(scan, engine)` among final rows; failed attempts are kept as non-final rows); the handler that records the last **required** final result completes the round under the message lock: it aggregates, sets `email_scans` (`scan_verdict`, status), moves `scanning → scanned` (E6) or `scan_failed` (E7) and writes `mail.message.scanned.v1` in the same transaction. A deadline sweeper finalizes rounds whose required engines have not all reported by `deadline_at` with `TIMEOUT` results.
2. **Rounds line up with the lifecycle.** The scan round is `email_messages.lifecycle_round`: `mail.message.parsed.v1` triggers round 0 (`scan_kind = initial`), `mail.message.rescan_requested.v1` carries its round (≥ 1, `scan_kind = rescan`). A rescan creates a new `email_scans` row and new results; nothing is deleted. Every transition carries `expectedRound`, so results of an older round never move a newer one.
3. **An engine registry.** The set of engines is data (`ENGINE_CATALOG` in `@email-gateway/security-contracts`: name, stage, queue, required by default, may a tenant disable it) plus one adapter per engine in the email-worker. The round snapshots its required engines (`email_scans.required_engines`) when it opens, so a configuration change never strands a round. Adding an engine (4.2–4.6) adds a catalog entry, an adapter and a queue binding; the orchestrator is unchanged. In 4.1 only `rspamd` is registered (required, not disableable). SPF/DKIM/DMARC/ARC come from the same Rspamd call, so `scan.auth` has no consumer and its bindings are removed (topology v3; the queue stays reserved).
4. **Standardized verdicts.** Every engine result is one of `clean, spam, suspicious, malicious, unknown, timeout, error` (stored lower-case; `skipped` remains for "not applicable", e.g. no attachments). `timeout` and `error` are failures: they never count as definite verdicts, and a round with a failed required engine ends `scan_failed`, never `scanned`. `unknown` is a definite answer without a classification (engine skipped the message, oversized, empty result) and is never treated as `clean`. The round's aggregate (`email_scans.scan_verdict`) is `malicious` if any engine said so, else `error`/`timeout` if a required engine failed, else the most severe definite verdict (`malicious > spam > suspicious > unknown > clean`). Each result keeps normalized reason codes, the engine's own signals (Rspamd symbols with score and options), the engine version and the settings version; authentication outcomes go to `email_auth_results`. Engines never choose the final action; policy (Phase 5) consumes `mail.message.scanned.v1` and the stored results.
5. **Thresholds are platform configuration with bounded per-tenant overrides** (`scan_engine_settings`, one validated JSON document per organization and engine, optimistic version). Tenants may move score thresholds inside platform bounds and may only raise the severity of the fixed rules (phishing, DMARC reject, authentication failures); they can never disable a required engine. The api validates and audits changes; the worker re-validates at use.
6. **Home: the email-worker, as a separately deployable scan role.** The scan stage runs in `apps/email-worker` behind role flags (`SCAN_ENABLED`, and the existing `MIME_INGESTION_ENABLED`), with its own RabbitMQ role user `eg-scanner`. Production runs two Deployments of the same image (MIME, scan), scaled independently on their queue lag; locally one container runs both roles.
7. **Resilience.** Per-call timeouts, an in-process circuit breaker per engine (open → immediate `error`/`circuit_open` attempt, no call), bounded concurrency (= prefetch), attempts recorded as non-final rows and retried through the step 3.2 `hold` retries until the engine's attempt budget or the round deadline, then a final `timeout`/`error`. Idempotency: the `processOnce` ledger per event plus the unique final-result key per `(message, round, engine)`.
8. **Development pass-through waits for the scan.** It now consumes `mail.message.scanned.v1` from the outbox and approves only `outcome = scanned` (new edge E27 `scanned → approved`, role `dev_passthrough`); `scan_failed` and `parse_failed` messages are never approved by it. E4 (`persisted → approved`) is retired.

**Alternatives considered.**

- _One orchestrator queue that fans out to engines and gathers results (saga)_: rejected. It needs a second hop per engine, a result queue and its own state machine, and the five scan queues bound by the brief's topology would stay unused; per-engine consumers with a completion check under the message lock give the same "all required engines reported" guarantee.
- _A central in-process fan-out (ADR-0007)_: rejected. One slow engine holds the whole message's prefetch slot and every engine must scale together.
- _A new `scanner` deployable_: rejected for now. ADR-0002/ADR-0007 and step 3.1 (M1 role flag) already place the engines in the email-worker; role flags give independent scaling with one image, and a separate broker user gives the least privilege a separate deployable would.
- _Rspamd consuming both `scan.auth` and `scan.spam`_: rejected (two identical Rspamd calls per message). _A separate first-party SPF/DKIM/DMARC engine_: rejected for 4.1 (Rspamd's modules already verify all four, with ARC).
- _Keeping the ADR-0007 outcome set and mapping spam to `suspicious`_: rejected (the brief fixes seven verdicts; policy needs to distinguish spam from uncertain mail).
- _Thresholds in policy rows (5.2)_: rejected for 4.1 (no policy engine yet); 5.2 may read the stored score and reason codes and apply its own rules on top.

**Consequences.** Every message passes through the scan stage before the dev pass-through (and later policy) can approve it, so end-to-end latency grows by one parse-to-scan hop. The scan tables gain columns and one new partitioned table (`email_auth_results`). Rspamd runs twice for inbound mail (SMTP-time milter, ADR-0027, and the authoritative post-queue `/checkv2` scan). A required engine that is not deployed makes every round `scan_failed` at its deadline, which is the intended fail-safe. Phase 5 policy must consume `mail.message.scanned.v1` (bound to `email.policy.evaluate`) and handle `scan_failed` (E9) explicitly.
