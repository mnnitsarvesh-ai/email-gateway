# ADR-0024: RabbitMQ topology v1, tenant routing keys, retry with backoff, dead-letter and replay

**Status:** Accepted (topology v2 by [ADR-0025](0025-message-lifecycle-state-machine.md); topology v3 by [ADR-0026](0026-security-scan-pipeline.md): `scan.spam` work queue, `scan.auth` unbound, `scanner` role user; topology v5 by [ADR-0029](0029-url-security-engine.md): `scan.url` work queue, `scan.url.inspect` and `scan.url.results`, `url-browser` role user; topology v6 by [ADR-0033](0033-bec-engine-and-stage-dependencies.md): `scan.bec` stage-2 work queue bound only to `mail.message.scan_stage_ready.v1`, scanner grants extended, 17 queues and 41 bindings — implemented and verified in step 4.4, [§13](../../plan/steps/12-phishing-bec.md#13-verification-results); topology v7 by [ADR-0036](0036-dlp-engine-and-extraction.md): `scan.dlp` becomes a stage-1 work queue, `mail.message.dlp_incident.v1` registered (no queue binds it yet), scanner grants extended to `scan.dlp`, still 17 queues and 41 bindings — implemented in step 4.5, [§13](../../plan/steps/13-dlp.md#13-verification-results); topology v8 by [ADR-0039](0039-sandbox-security-boundary-and-backends.md)/[ADR-0040](0040-sandbox-engine-dispatch-results-artifacts.md): `scan.sandbox` becomes the sandbox-controller's bounded lossy work queue bound to `mail.sandbox.task_requested.v1` (qualified retry key), new work queues `scan.sandbox.submissions` and `scan.sandbox.results`, role user `sandbox-controller` (`eg-sandbox`), 19 queues and 45 bindings — implemented in step 4.6, [§13](../../plan/steps/14-sandbox.md#13-verification-results)). Supersedes [ADR-0005](0005-rabbitmq-outbox-idempotency.md) (its transactional outbox, `processOnce` ledger and envelope decisions are carried forward unchanged in decision 1; its topology — `mail.events`, `mail.dlx`, `<queue>.dlq` — is replaced). Works with [ADR-0023](0023-outbox-relay-and-reconciliation.md) (relay). Design: [step 3.2](../../plan/steps/07-rabbitmq.md).

**Context.** The product brief fixes the broker topology: six exchanges (`email.ingestion`, `email.security`, `email.policy`, `email.delivery`, `email.archive`, `email.events`) and fourteen queues (`email.received`, `scan.auth`, `scan.spam`, `scan.url`, `scan.attachment`, `scan.dlp`, `scan.sandbox`, `email.policy.evaluate`, `email.delivery.pending`, `email.delivery.retry`, `email.quarantine`, `email.archive`, `email.analytics`, `email.dead-letter`), with durable publishing, confirms, manual acks, idempotent consumers, retry with exponential backoff, dead-lettering, monitoring, tenant-aware routing, correlation ids, schema versioning and backpressure. ADR-0005's single-exchange topology was never fed (no relay existed) and does not match.

**Decision.**

1. **Carried forward from ADR-0005:** transactional outbox (`message_outbox`), `processOnce` idempotency ledger (`message_idempotency`) in the same transaction as the effects, the envelope (`eventId` UUIDv7, versioned `eventType`, `occurredAt`, `correlationId`, `organizationId`, `payload`), payloads with ids, S3 keys and metadata only, breaking changes as a new `.vN` with both versions consumed during migration.
2. **Topology as versioned data.** `packages/event-contracts/src/topology.ts` (`TOPOLOGY_VERSION = 1`) declares exactly the six exchanges (topic, durable) and fourteen queues (all quorum, durable) with bindings, policies and retry policies; `infrastructure/rabbitmq/definitions.json` is generated from it (a test fails on drift) and imported idempotently into the dedicated vhost `email-gateway` by `infrastructure/rabbitmq/init.mjs` (Compose `rabbitmq-init`, `pnpm rabbitmq:init`). Mutable queue settings (delivery limit, dead-letter target, overflow, length limits) are **policies**, so they change without re-declaring queues. Services never declare: they verify passively at startup and readiness.
3. **Routing keys carry the tenant.** Events: `org.<organizationId>.<eventType>` (e.g. `org.42.mail.message.received.v1`), bindings `org.*.<eventType>`, so per-tenant queues are a binding change later. Internal hops: `retry.<queue>.org.<id>.<eventType>` (back into one queue), `delay.<queue>.org.<id>.<eventType>` (into its delay queue), `dead-letter.<queue>[.org.<id>.<eventType>]` (into `email.dead-letter`). Every non-`email.events` exchange is bound to `email.events` with `org.#`, so `email.events` is the firehose (analytics) and never sees internal hops.
4. **Headers and properties.** `messageId = eventId`, `correlationId`, `type = eventType`, `timestamp`, `deliveryMode = 2`, `contentType = application/json`, headers `x-eg-envelope-version`, `x-eg-event-type`, `x-eg-schema-version` (the `N` of `.vN`), `x-eg-organization-id`, `x-eg-correlation-id`, plus retry and failure headers. Consumers refuse an unknown event type or schema version for their queue and a tenant mismatch between routing key, header and envelope (dead-lettered, never retried).
5. **Content guard.** Every publish (relay, retry, dead-letter, replay) passes `assertMetadataOnly`: no binary values, no serialized buffers, no strings over 2 048 characters, no MIME-looking or base64-blob strings, no body/content-named fields, envelope ≤ 128 KiB; the broker additionally caps `max_message_size` at 1 MiB.
6. **Active vs dormant queues.** Queues with a consumer in this step (`email.received`, `email.delivery.pending`) use the work policy: `delivery-limit` 20 (crash-loop backstop), dead-letter to `email.events`/`dead-letter.<queue>` with `at-least-once` strategy, `overflow: reject-publish` (a full queue nacks the publish and the outbox absorbs the backlog). Queues of stages not built yet are bound but **dormant**: no consumer, `max-length` 10 000 with `overflow: drop-head`, so an unconsumed stage can never block the pipeline or exhaust the broker. A stage's own step switches its queue to the work policy and onboards its backlog from MySQL, not from the queue.
7. **Retry in the consumer library, attempts in headers.** A handler failure is classified: _fatal_ (contract, tenant, `NonRetryableError`) → dead-letter now; _transient_ (database, S3, network, timeouts) → retried with backoff **without** consuming an attempt, bounded by `maxRetryAgeMs`; _retryable_ (anything else) → `x-eg-attempt + 1`, dead-letter after `maxAttempts`. Backoff is exponential with equal jitter: `d = min(max, base·2^(n−1))`, delay ∈ [d/2, d]. Two strategies, chosen per queue in the topology: **`delay-queue`** (delivery: republish to `email.delivery.retry` with a per-message TTL; on expiry the broker dead-letters it back to `email.delivery.pending`) and **`hold`** (other queues: the message keeps its prefetch slot for the delay, ≤ 60 s, then a copy is republished to the tail with `retry.<queue>` and the original acked). A retry copy is confirmed before the original is acked; a crash in between yields a duplicate, absorbed by `processOnce`.
8. **Dead-letter.** `email.dead-letter` (quorum, no consumer) receives consumer-published dead letters with `x-eg-failure-reason`, `x-eg-failure-class`, `x-eg-source-queue`, `x-eg-attempts`, `x-eg-dead-lettered-at`, `x-eg-dead-lettered-by`, and broker dead letters (delivery limit) with `x-death`/`x-first-death-*`. Reasons are codes, never error messages.
9. **Replay** is a platform API: `POST /api/v1/platform/dead-letters/replay` (permission `platform:dead_letter_replay`, MFA, users only, audited in `audit_logs`). It takes up to `limit` dead letters matching a source queue and optional filters, republishes each unchanged envelope to `retry.<sourceQueue>` with fresh retry headers and a `x-eg-replay-id`, confirmed, then acks it in the dead-letter queue; non-matching messages are returned to the queue. Replaying an already processed event is a ledger duplicate, so replay is safe to repeat.
10. **Least privilege.** Dedicated vhost `email-gateway`; users per role with `configure = ^$`: `eg-relay` (write: the six exchanges), `eg-email-worker` (read `email.received`; write `email.ingestion`, `email.events`), `eg-delivery-worker` (read `email.delivery.pending`; write `email.delivery`, `email.events`), `eg-ops` (read `email.dead-letter`; write the six exchanges). The administrator account is used only by provisioning, tests and operators. Passwords come only from `.env`/secrets.
11. **Client: amqplib 0.10 plus our own recovery layer** in `@email-gateway/common` (`messaging/`): reconnect with jittered backoff across a list of node URLs, channel re-creation and consumer re-subscription, blocked-connection handling, confirm channels with per-message confirm timeouts and mandatory returns, manual acks only, prefetch-bounded concurrency.

**Alternatives.**

- _Per-queue delay queues (`<queue>.retry` × 13) with TTL tiers:_ the textbook approach, but it adds queues outside the brief's fixed list. Kept for delivery only, where the brief names `email.delivery.retry`.
- _Delayed-message exchange plugin:_ not in the official image, a seventh exchange, delayed messages live on one node (lost with it). Rejected.
- _Broker `x-delivery-limit` as the only retry:_ no backoff, and infrastructure outages would dead-letter healthy messages. Kept only as a crash-loop backstop.
- _amqp-connection-manager / Rascal / @golevelup/nestjs-rabbitmq:_ in-memory publish buffering while disconnected hides failures from the relay (a row must stay unpublished until confirmed), and configuration-driven topology duplicates `topology.ts`. Rejected for a small, tested recovery layer on amqplib, which the repository already uses.
- _One exchange with type-based routing keys (ADR-0005):_ does not match the brief and carries no tenant. Superseded.

**Consequences.** Adding a stage consumer means: a ledger consumer name, a policy switch from dormant to work, a user/permission change in `init.mjs`, and a reconciliation entry. Dormant queues are lossy by design until their stage exists. The `hold` strategy keeps prefetch slots busy during an outage, which is the intended backpressure but caps a single delay at 60 s. A 3-node cluster is required for quorum replication; locally it exists only in the `rabbitmq-cluster` Compose profile for the node-failure test, and the production broker topology (Amazon MQ cluster or self-managed) is decided in 10.1.

**Amendment (step 3.2 review round 1, 2026-11-04).**

- _Delivery limit of queues no stage consumes._ The dead-letter, delay and dormant policies set `delivery-limit: -1`. RabbitMQ 4.x applies a default limit of 20 to a quorum queue whose policy sets none and drops a message over it when the queue has no dead-letter target; replay scans return every message they do not take with `nack(requeue)`, so dead letters disappeared after about 20 scans. Work queues keep `delivery-limit: 20` with their dead-letter target (crash-loop backstop).
- _The broker is a trust boundary for approvals._ Topic permissions now restrict the routing keys each role user may publish: only `eg-relay` may publish event keys (`org.<id>.<type>`); a consumer may publish the retry/delay/dead-letter hops of its own queue only; `eg-ops` (api) may publish the retry hops of the two replayable queues only, and its exchange write is narrowed to `email.ingestion` and `email.delivery`. Because a retry key reaches its work queue, the broker alone cannot stop a leaked `eg-delivery-worker` or `eg-ops` credential, so the delivery planner plans only approvals the gateway issued (a `message_outbox` row with the same event id, type, organization and payload; else a fatal dead letter `approval_not_issued` / `approval_mismatch`). Residual risk, accepted: such a credential can re-send a genuinely issued approval (a ledger duplicate) or fill a queue. Approvals older than the outbox retention (7 days) can no longer be proven and are dead-lettered (fail closed).
