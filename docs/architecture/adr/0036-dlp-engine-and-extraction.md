# ADR-0036: DLP engine on `scan.dlp` as a stage-1 engine; attachment text extraction in the isolated inspector child (topology v7)

**Status:** Accepted; implemented in step 4.5 (implementation notes below). Design: [step 4.5](../../plan/steps/13-dlp.md) D1–D4, D10. Amends [ADR-0024](0024-rabbitmq-topology-retry-dead-letter.md) (topology v7: `scan.dlp` becomes a work queue, `mail.message.dlp_incident.v1` registered), [ADR-0026](0026-security-scan-pipeline.md) (the `dlp` engine registered) and [ADR-0028](0028-attachment-scanning-engine.md) (the inspector pool and the archive walker are shared with the DLP extractor).

**Context.** Step 4.5 adds Data Loss Prevention: detection of card numbers, bank identifiers, national ids, passports, personal data, confidential keywords, source code, credentials, financial documents and tenant patterns, in message bodies **and attachment text**, with outbound policy actions before delivery approval. The scan pipeline (ADR-0026, ADR-0033) runs engines of one round in parallel on their own queues; stage-2 engines run after their dependencies. Topology v6 has a dormant `scan.dlp` bound to `parsed.v1` and `rescan_requested.v1`. The attachment engine (ADR-0028) already walks zip/gzip/tar (OOXML/ODF as zip) with strict limits inside a resource-limited child process; it stores no text. The local VM has 8 GB.

**Decision.**

1. **One `dlp` engine on `scan.dlp`, stage 1** (no `dependsOn`), required for every round, `tenantMayDisable: false`. It reads stored parts and attachment bytes itself and depends on no other engine's verdict. Outbound and internal mail are always scanned; inbound only when the tenant enables it (else a definite `skipped`). Because it is required, the round — and therefore delivery approval — waits for the DLP final and its policy action.
2. **Topology v7:** `scan.dlp` becomes a work queue (ledger consumer `email-worker.scan.dlp`, plain retry binding, hold retry strategy), 17 queues, 41 bindings (unchanged: the dormant v1–v6 `scan.dlp` already carried its retry binding; step 4.5 §12 WP1 deviation 1); `eg-scanner` gains its grants; `mail.message.dlp_incident.v1` is registered without a queue binding (analytics firehose now, notification-worker in 6.2).
3. **Extraction reuses 4.2, not duplicates it:** the `InspectorPool` is generalized to a child descriptor (entry, arguments, handshake validator) and the zip/gzip/tar walker gains an optional entry visitor; the attachment engine's behaviour and content version are unchanged. A second pool runs a **DLP extractor child** (no YARA) with the same isolation (empty environment, permission model, heap/RSS/CPU limits, wall clock, recycling) and the same platform archive limits, so DLP reaches exactly the entries the attachment engine reaches. Formats: text/CSV, HTML (converted in the worker), PDF via `pdfjs-dist` with evaluation disabled, OOXML and ODF via an own bounded XML tokenizer without DTD processing. Images and unsupported formats are recorded as not extracted.
4. **Never clean by accident:** every unextracted attachment, truncated source or partial custom-pattern scan is a coverage reason code; the verdict is `unknown` without findings, `suspicious` with findings, `clean` only with complete coverage. The engine outcome is never `malicious` or `spam`.

**Alternatives considered.** Stage-2 after `attachment` (latency of ClamAV/YARA, failure coupling, no stored text to consume); text stored in S3 by the attachment engine (second copy of content, breaks its tenant-independent cache); an Apache Tika container (JVM memory on an 8 GB VM, a content-carrying network hop); extraction in the worker's main process (hostile documents could exhaust the scan role); an own PDF parser (poor CID/ToUnicode coverage).

**Consequences.** A fifth required engine: every round waits for DLP (fast `skipped` for inbound by default); outbound latency grows by extraction time (benchmarked). Mail with image attachments is `unknown` from DLP unless all text is found — honest, and harmless for delivery unless a tenant rule selects that coverage reason. The email-worker image grows by pdfjs-dist; no new container. Rollout is two-phase (code first, then `SCAN_ENGINES`), as in 4.4.

**Implementation notes (step 4.5, recorded in [§12](../../plan/steps/13-dlp.md#12-deviations-log) and verified in [§13](../../plan/steps/13-dlp.md#13-verification-results)).** The decisions hold. Details that differ from or refine the design text, each with evidence in §12: (a) topology v7 keeps **41 bindings**, not 42: the dormant `scan.dlp` already had its retry binding (WP1 deviation 1); (b) the generalized pool is `ChildPool` with a `ChildDescriptor` (`InspectorPool` and `DlpExtractorPool` are instances); the walker's entry visitor has an error contract (a visitor error aborts the walk in every container format; closer review); attachment results stay byte-identical; (c) an exception inside the extractor while extracting is a definite `parse_error` for that file, and a child that cannot load pdfjs never becomes ready (WP3 deviation 10); (d) inbound `skipped` rounds also write a `dlp_scan_results` row (WP3 deviation 5); (e) the attachments of one message are extracted sequentially, bounded by the byte budget across messages (WP3 deviation 13); (f) the 4 MiB text target is met at concurrency 1 but not at 4, because detection runs on the worker's event loop (WP3 deviation 16; scaling is by replicas). Not verified: the Kubernetes manifests were rendered, never applied; OCR and legacy Office formats are out of scope.
