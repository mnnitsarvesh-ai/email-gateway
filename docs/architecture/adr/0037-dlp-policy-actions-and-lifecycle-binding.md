# ADR-0037: DLP policies evaluated at scan time; five actions bound to the lifecycle through the delivery stage

**Status:** Accepted; implemented in step 4.5 for the engine and the development pass-through (implementation notes below); the binding for policy 5.2 / quarantine 5.3 (decision 6) is a contract for those steps. Design: [step 4.5](../../plan/steps/13-dlp.md) D11–D14. Binds the future policy engine (step 5.2) and quarantine (step 5.3); does not change [ADR-0025](0025-message-lifecycle-state-machine.md).

**Context.** The brief requires tenant DLP rules with the actions ALLOW, ALERT, QUARANTINE, BLOCK and REQUIRE_APPROVAL, exceptions, and outbound policy evaluation before delivery approval. The policy engine (5.2) and quarantine (5.3) do not exist yet; hold states (`quarantined`, `held_for_review`, `rejected`) are reachable only through `policy_evaluated` with role `policy`. The development pass-through (local/ci) approves `scanned` messages and already holds failed scans, malicious verdicts and deterministic BEC suspicious/malicious results by not approving them.

**Decision.**

1. **Evaluation at scan time, inside the `dlp` engine**, by a pure, deterministic function of the findings, coverage, direction, envelope, the tenant's policy snapshot, exceptions and the time: rules sorted by (policy priority, policy id, rule priority, rule id); the effective action is the **strongest** matched action of enforce-mode rules in the order `allow < alert < require_approval < quarantine < block`; the winner is the first rule with that action; monitor-mode matches are recorded only; `allow` rules never override (allowing is done with exceptions). Without an enabled tenant policy a platform default applies (`alert` on high-severity card, bank, national id, passport and credential findings; tenant-switchable to `allow`). The result row records the action, the winning rule, matched rules, applied exceptions and a policy version hash.
2. **Exceptions** (sender/recipient address or domain, optionally limited to a policy, rule, detectors or categories) require a reason and an expiry (≤ 365 days) and are audited; applied exceptions are recorded per result. With a truncated recipient list recipient exceptions are not applied (never weaker). **Trust model:** sender exceptions and rule `sender` conditions match the envelope sender (MAIL FROM), lower-cased. It is not bound to an authenticated user: outbound, the gateway authenticates only the organization's connector and checks the sender domain, so anyone who can submit through the connector can use an exempted address; inbound, MAIL FROM is attacker-controlled. The effect stays within one tenant, but a sender exception can weaken the outbound control, so recipient and domain exceptions are preferred. Binding sender exceptions to the header From or an authenticated login is left for 5.2 (security review of 2026-11-08).
3. **Message-level action:** the strongest rule over all recipients applies to the message; per-recipient splits are 5.2's.
4. **Alert** = a `mail.message.dlp_incident.v1` outbox row written with the final (also for the hold actions) plus the incidents API; the notification-worker (6.2) binds the event.
5. **Application by the delivery stage:** the dev pass-through approves `allow` and `alert`, and **does not approve** `require_approval`, `quarantine`, `block` or a missing result row (`delivery_approvals_skipped_total{reason="dlp_action"}`); the message stays `scanned` until policy or its deadline. No new lifecycle edge.
6. **Binding for 5.2/5.3:** policy reads the round's `dlp_scan_results` row and may be **stricter, never weaker**: `allow`/`alert` → `policy_deliver` (E8 → E10); `require_approval` → `policy_hold` (E12, released by an approver, E15); `quarantine` → `policy_quarantine` (E11); `block` → `policy_reject` (E13) with a non-delivery notice to the internal sender. A missing row for a definite, non-skipped dlp final is a hold.

**Alternatives considered.** Evaluation only in 5.2 (does not exist; the brief needs it now); new dev pass-through edges into hold states (anticipates 5.3 and changes the ADR-0025 role model); `allow` rules that override stronger rules (order-dependent surprises; exceptions are explicit and expire); per-recipient actions now (needs split approvals).

**Consequences.** The DLP action is decided once per round, explainable and reproducible from the recorded versions; a policy change applies to new rounds (rescans) and within the worker's tenant cache TTL. Locally, held messages expire through the `scanned` deadline until 5.2/5.3 provide release and rejection paths.

**Implementation notes (step 4.5, recorded in [§12](../../plan/steps/13-dlp.md#12-deviations-log) and verified in [§13](../../plan/steps/13-dlp.md#13-verification-results)).** The decisions hold. Refinements, with evidence in §12: (a) snapshot ids are public ids (UUIDv7) in the stored `policy` JSON and the incident; only `matched_policy_id`/`matched_rule_id` hold internal ids; ties are broken on the public id (WP3 deviation 1); (b) the incident event requires `tenant_policy` ⇔ both ids set and `default_policy` ⇔ both null (WP1 deviation 2); (c) **a coverage-only match** (a rule selecting `coverageReasons` without a finding) writes an incident with no category: `mail.message.dlp_incident.v1` allows an empty `categories` list only with `coverage: partial`, and `detectorCodes` then holds the coverage codes (WP3 deviation 4, closed by the closer review of 2026-11-08); (d) a missing `dlp_scan_results` row for a definite non-skipped dlp final is held by the pass-through and counted as `delivery_dlp_holds_total{action="missing"}` (WP4 deviation 8); (e) exceptions change only reason, expiry, enabled and filters after creation (WP4 deviation 4). The stack suite `dlp-smtp` verifies quarantine, block and require_approval held, alert and an exception-allowed message delivered, and the internal-direction rule.
