# Messaging topology (RabbitMQ, topology v6)

The reference for the RabbitMQ broker objects of the gateway: exchanges, queues, bindings, routing keys, headers, policies, dead-letter paths, retry hops, the dormant-queue rule, users and permissions, and provisioning. Decisions: [ADR-0024](adr/0024-rabbitmq-topology-retry-dead-letter.md) (topology, retry, dead-letter, replay, least privilege; supersedes ADR-0005) and [ADR-0023](adr/0023-outbox-relay-and-reconciliation.md) (outbox relay and reconciliation). Design: [step 3.2](../plan/steps/07-rabbitmq.md) (§ and Q references below point there).

**Topology v6 (step 4.4, [design B2/B3](../plan/steps/12-phishing-bec.md#b3-topology-v6-and-grants), [ADR-0033](adr/0033-bec-engine-and-stage-dependencies.md)).** `TOPOLOGY_VERSION = 6`; the six exchanges are unchanged, the queues are **seventeen** (one new), the bindings 41 (v5: 39):

- **`scan.bec`** (new, work, home `email.security`): the phishing/BEC engine of the email-worker scan role (ledger consumer `email-worker.scan.bec`, broker user `eg-scanner`), the work policy and retry `hold`, 10 attempts, 2 s–60 s, 24 h (the engine's budget `SCAN_BEC_MAX_ATTEMPTS`, 3, finalizes first). It is the first **stage-2** engine queue: bound only to `org.*.mail.message.scan_stage_ready.v1` + `retry.scan.bec.#`, never to `parsed.v1` or `rescan_requested.v1`. `scan.bec` is no dot-prefix of another queue, so its retry binding is the plain form. Description "Phishing and BEC (step 4.4): stage 2, triggered when rspamd and url finalized".
- **`mail.message.scan_stage_ready.v1` is registered** (exchange `email.security`, aggregate `message`, producer the email-worker scan stage through `message_outbox`): written exactly once per (round, stage-2 engine) in the `ScanRepository.complete()` transaction that inserts the last dependency final of the round's required set (both locks held). Strict payload ([below](#mailmessagescan_stage_readyv1-step-44)); routed to `scan.bec` and, like every `email.security` key, to the `email.analytics` firehose (ids, codes and the S3 key only).
- Engines may declare `dependsOn` in the catalog (`@email-gateway/security-contracts`, `validateCatalog`: known engines, no self dependency, depth 1). The `bec` catalog entry depends on `rspamd` and `url`.
- Provisioning: **`eg-scanner`** reads `^scan\.(spam|attachment|url|url\.results|bec)$` and publishes `^retry\.scan\.(spam|attachment|url|url\.results|bec)\.org\.` on `email.security` and `^dead-letter\.scan\.(spam|attachment|url|url\.results|bec)(\.org\.|$)` on `email.events` (the `.org.` anchoring of v5 kept for every scanner queue). **`eg-ops`** may replay `scan.bec` (`^retry\.scan\.(spam|attachment|url|url\.inspect|url\.results|bec)\.`). The relay needs nothing new (it already publishes `^org\.` keys to `email.security`); the scanner never publishes the stage trigger itself. Vhost description `email-gateway topology v6`; six role users.
- The reconciler may track `scan.bec` (`RECONCILE_QUEUES=…,scan.url,scan.bec`, env only; it derives the event type from the binding). Dead-letter replay covers `scan.bec` automatically (`REPLAYABLE_QUEUES` is derived from the work queues).
- Upgrading a broker: `pnpm rabbitmq:init` (or the Compose `rabbitmq-init`). The import adds the queue, its two bindings and its policy; nothing is pruned. Until the BEC engine runs (step 4.4 WP3), nothing publishes `scan_stage_ready.v1`, so `scan.bec` stays empty.

**Topology v5 (step 4.3, [design U2](../plan/steps/11-url-protection.md#u2-topology-v5-and-the-three-url-events), [ADR-0029](adr/0029-url-security-engine.md)).** `TOPOLOGY_VERSION = 5`; the six exchanges are unchanged, the queues are **sixteen** (two new), the bindings 39 (v4: 35):

- `scan.url` becomes a **work** queue: the URL engine of the email-worker scan role (ledger consumer `email-worker.scan.url`, broker user `eg-scanner`), the work policy and retry `hold`, 10 attempts, 2 s–60 s, 24 h (the engine's budget `SCAN_URL_MAX_ATTEMPTS`, 6, finalizes first). Event bindings unchanged (`parsed.v1`, `rescan_requested.v1`). Its retry binding becomes the **qualified `retry.scan.url.org.#`** (was `retry.scan.url.#`): `scan.url` is now a dot-prefix of the two new queue names, and the unqualified pattern would also match their retry hops `retry.scan.url.inspect.org.…` / `retry.scan.url.results.org.…` (every retry key is `retry.<queue>.org.<id>.<type>`, so the qualified form matches exactly `scan.url`'s own hops; `retryBindingKey()` derives it, `validateTopology` and the tests prove every retry hop of every queue reaches exactly that queue). `init.mjs` prunes the old binding.
- **`scan.url.inspect`** (new, work, home `email.security`): bound to `org.*.mail.url.inspection_requested.v1` + `retry.scan.url.inspect.#`, consumed by the isolated **url-browser-worker** (broker user `eg-url-browser`) through the new consumer kind **`stateless`** (`{ kind: 'stateless', service, reason }`: a service without a database, hence no `message_idempotency` ledger; its results are deduplicated by `inspectionId` in `scan.url.results`; the reconciler never re-drives a stateless queue). `validateTopology` allows `stateless` only on work queues. Retry `hold`, 3 attempts, 5 s–60 s, 1 h. Policy **`work-lossy`** (`policyFor('work-lossy', …)`, flag `lossy: true` on the definition, work queues only): `delivery-limit 20`, dead-letter `dead-letter.scan.url.inspect` like a work queue, but `max-length 10 000` with **`overflow drop-head`** and `dead-letter-strategy at-most-once` (see [the lossy work queue](#the-lossy-work-queue-v5) below).
- **`scan.url.results`** (new, work, home `email.security`): bound to `org.*.mail.url.inspection_completed.v1` + `retry.scan.url.results.#`, ledger consumer `email-worker.scan.url.results` (scan role, `eg-scanner`), retry `hold`, 10, 2 s–60 s, 24 h, the work policy.
- **Three URL events registered** (exchange `email.security`, aggregate `message`; [URL events](#url-events-step-43) below): `mail.url.inspection_requested.v1` (email-worker URL engine, outbox), `mail.url.inspection_completed.v1` (url-browser-worker, **direct publish with publisher confirms**: it has no database, so no outbox) and `mail.url.clicked.v1` (click-service, outbox; no queue binding, it reaches only `email.analytics`).
- Provisioning: **`eg-scanner`** reads `^scan\.(spam|attachment|url|url\.results)$` and publishes `^retry\.scan\.(spam|attachment|url|url\.results)\.org\.` on `email.security` and `^dead-letter\.scan\.(spam|attachment|url|url\.results)(\.org\.|$)` on `email.events` (anchored on `.org.` so the `url` alternative never covers the hops of `scan.url.inspect`). New role user **`eg-url-browser`** (`RABBITMQ_URL_BROWSER_USER` / `RABBITMQ_URL_BROWSER_PASSWORD`): configure nothing, read `^scan\.url\.inspect$`, write `email.security` keys `^org\.[1-9][0-9]{0,19}\.mail\.url\.inspection_completed\.v1$` (its only event key: the relay is no longer the only publisher of an `org.` key, but this user can mint nothing else) and `^retry\.scan\.url\.inspect\.`, and `email.events` `^dead-letter\.scan\.url\.inspect(\.|$)`. **`eg-ops`** may replay the three new work queues (`^retry\.scan\.(spam|attachment|url|url\.inspect|url\.results)\.`). The click-service has no broker user. Vhost description `email-gateway topology v5`; six role users.
- The reconciler may track `scan.url` (`RECONCILE_QUEUES=…,scan.url`, env only). Dead-letter replay covers the three new work queues automatically (`REPLAYABLE_QUEUES` is derived from the work queues).
- Upgrading a broker: `pnpm rabbitmq:init` (or the Compose `rabbitmq-init`) after the `.env` has the url-browser keys. The import replaces the `eg.scan.url` policy (dormant drop-head 10 000 → work reject-publish 1 000 000), creates the two queues and prunes `retry.scan.url.#`. Messages a v4 `scan.url` buffered stay and are consumed by the URL engine once it runs (stale rounds have no effect). Until the URL engine runs, `scan.url` accumulates every `parsed.v1`/`rescan_requested.v1` under the work policy (as `scan.attachment` did in step 4.2); test suites no longer purge it (it left `DORMANT_QUEUES` in `tests/integration/support/broker.ts`).

Bindings unchanged (`parsed.v1`, `rescan_requested.v1`, `retry.scan.attachment.#`). Description "Attachments: file type, known hashes, ClamAV, YARA, archive limits (step 4.2)".
- **`mail.attachment.sandbox_requested.v1` is registered** (it was reserved since step 3.2): exchange `email.security`, outbox `aggregate_type = 'attachment'` (`aggregate_id` = `sandbox_submissions.id`), producer the email-worker's attachment engine, written in the finalize transaction of its final result together with the `sandbox_submissions` row; strict payload (ids, `receivedAt`, `direction`, `scanId`, `lifecycleRound`, `attachment { partIndex, sha256, sizeBytes, detectedType, objectKey }` with an `org/<id>/part/…` key, `attachmentVerdict` `suspicious` | `unknown`, ≤ 16 reason codes, `requestedAt`; no filename, no content). `RESERVED_EVENT_TYPES` is now empty (kept for future reservations). `scan.sandbox` stays **dormant** (planned 4.6), binding unchanged: it buffers the requests (drop-head, 10 000) until step 4.6.

The scan round never waits for it.
- Provisioning: **`eg-scanner`** reads `^scan\.(spam|attachment)$` and publishes `^retry\.scan\.(spam|attachment)\.` on `email.security` and `^dead-letter\.scan\.(spam|attachment)(\.|$)` on `email.events`; **`eg-ops`** may replay `scan.attachment` dead letters (topic `^retry\.scan\.(spam|attachment)\.` on `email.security`). The scanner never publishes `sandbox_requested.v1` itself (outbox → relay, whose user already publishes `^org\.` keys to `email.security`). `init.mjs --users` sets and verifies the new grants exactly; the vhost description is `email-gateway topology v4`.
- The reconciler may track `scan.attachment` (`RECONCILE_QUEUES=email.received,email.delivery.pending,scan.spam,scan.attachment`, env only). Dead-letter replay covers `scan.attachment` automatically (`REPLAYABLE_QUEUES` is derived from the work queues; the api's replay route description lists it).

The import replaces the `eg.scan.attachment` policy (dormant drop-head 10 000 → work reject-publish 1 000 000 with its dead-letter target); messages a v3 `scan.attachment` buffered stay in the queue and are consumed by the attachment engine once it runs (rounds whose message has moved on are stale, no effect). Test suites must no longer purge `scan.attachment` (it left `DORMANT_QUEUES` in `tests/integration/support/broker.ts`).

## Source of truth and provisioning

- **`packages/event-contracts/src/registry.ts`**: `EVENT_REGISTRY` (every concrete event type `<name>.vN` → exchange, zod schema, outbox `aggregate_type`, version), `RESERVED_EVENT_TYPES`, `eventDefinition(type)`, `schemaVersionOf(type)`.
- **`packages/event-contracts/src/topology.ts`**: `TOPOLOGY` (`TOPOLOGY_VERSION = 6`, `DEFAULT_VHOST = 'email-gateway'`), `EXCHANGE_NAMES`, `QUEUE_NAMES`, per-queue role, home exchange, bindings, consumer (`ledger`, `stateless` (v5), `planned`, `none`), retry policy, `lossy` flag (v5) and policy definition (`policyFor(kind)`, `policyKindOf(queue)`: the four roles plus `work-lossy`); helpers `queueDefinition`, `retryBindingKey` (v5), `eventRoutingKey`, `retryRoutingKey`, `delayRoutingKey`, `deadLetterRoutingKey`, `parseRoutingKey`, `eventTypesBoundTo`, `routeMessage`/`topicMatches` (AMQP topic semantics), `retryPolicyViolations`, `validateTopology` (the §3 invariants, run by the tests and by the export CLI).
- **`packages/event-contracts/src/definitions.ts`**: `toRabbitDefinitions(topology, { vhost })` → the body of `POST /api/definitions/{vhost}` plus `topology_version`. During the step 3.2 transition the package root still exports the ADR-0005 names (`EXCHANGES`, `QUEUES`, `QueueDefinition`, …) from `legacy-topology.ts`; topology v1's queue type is exported from the root as `TopologyQueueDefinition` until WP4 deletes the legacy file.
- **`infrastructure/rabbitmq/definitions.json`**: generated, committed. `pnpm --filter @email-gateway/event-contracts topology:export` rewrites it (build, export, Prettier); a unit test fails when the committed file differs from the generated one. Never edit it by hand.
- **`infrastructure/rabbitmq/init.mjs`**: the only provisioning code (plain Node 22, no dependencies, the management HTTP API as the administrator). It waits for the API (≤ 120 s); creates/updates the vhost (description `email-gateway topology v<N>`, `default_queue_type: quorum`) and gives the administrator full permissions on it; imports the definitions (without `topology_version`, every object's `vhost` set to the target); **prunes** every binding whose destination is a queue or exchange of the definitions and that the definitions do not declare (v3; objects outside the topology are never touched; names and counts are logged; `--prune-dry-run` only lists them); with `--users` creates/updates the six role users (no tags) and sets exactly their permissions; with `--cleanup-legacy` deletes the ADR-0005 objects on vhost `/` (below) only when empty; then **verifies**: exactly the 6 exchanges (topic, durable, not internal) and 17 queues (quorum, durable), each queue's effective policy is `eg.<queue>`, every binding present and no other binding, every policy equal to the file, the role users without tags and with exactly their permissions. Idempotent; exits non-zero on any failure; logs object names and counts only. `--vhost <name>` provisions another vhost (integration suites use `eg-it-<hex>`), `--mgmt-url <url>` another broker, `--definitions <path>` another file, `--delete-vhost <name>` tears a test vhost down (refuses `email-gateway` and `/`).
- **Compose** `rabbitmq-init` (default profile, `node:22-bookworm-slim`, mounts `infrastructure/rabbitmq` read-only, `--users --cleanup-legacy`, after `rabbitmq` is healthy); every app service waits for it (`service_completed_successfully`). **Host:** `pnpm rabbitmq:init` (`node --env-file=.env infrastructure/rabbitmq/init.mjs --users`, management URL from `RABBITMQ_MGMT_URL`).
- **Services never declare** (their users have `configure = ^$`). The client library (WP2) verifies passively (`checkExchange`/`checkQueue`) the exchanges a service publishes to and the queues it consumes; a missing object keeps readiness red, it does not crash the process.
- An import never deletes. Since v3 `init.mjs` removes stale **bindings** on topology objects itself (the prune step); a queue, exchange or policy removed from `TOPOLOGY` must still be deleted explicitly (the verification reports leftover queues and exchanges as unexpected, leftover policies as a warning). Topology changes are a new `TOPOLOGY_VERSION` with a migration note.

Defined by Q2; the constants (`HEADERS`) and the codec are built by **step 3.2 WP2** (`packages/common/src/messaging/headers.ts`, `codec.ts`).

## Policies

One policy per queue, `eg.<queue>`, pattern `^<escaped queue name>$`, `apply-to: queues`, priority 10, imported with the definitions. Services cannot change policies (no `policymaker`/`administrator` tag).

| Role            | Queues                                                                                                                   | Definition                                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| --------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| work            | `email.received`, `scan.spam`, `scan.attachment`, `scan.url`, `scan.url.results`, `scan.bec`, `email.delivery.pending`   | `delivery-limit: 20`, `dead-letter-exchange: email.events`, `dead-letter-routing-key: dead-letter.<queue>`, `dead-letter-strategy: at-least-once`, `overflow: reject-publish`, `max-length: 1000000`                    |
| work-lossy (v5) | `scan.url.inspect`                                                                                                       | `delivery-limit: 20`, `dead-letter-exchange: email.events`, `dead-letter-routing-key: dead-letter.<queue>`, `dead-letter-strategy: at-most-once`, `overflow: drop-head`, `max-length: 10000`                            |
| dormant         | `scan.auth`, `scan.dlp`, `scan.sandbox`, `email.policy.evaluate`, `email.quarantine`, `email.archive`, `email.analytics` | `delivery-limit: -1`, `max-length: 10000`, `overflow: drop-head` (no dead-lettering)                                                                                                                                    |
| delay           | `email.delivery.retry`                                                                                                   | `delivery-limit: -1`, `dead-letter-exchange: email.delivery`, `dead-letter-routing-key: retry.email.delivery.pending.expired`, `dead-letter-strategy: at-least-once`, `overflow: reject-publish`, `max-length: 1000000` |
| dead-letter     | `email.dead-letter`                                                                                                      | `delivery-limit: -1`, `overflow: reject-publish`, `max-length: 1000000`                                                                                                                                                 |

- `delivery-limit` is a crash-loop backstop only: application retries republish copies (a copy is a new message with its own count). Returns of the original do count: every `nack(requeue)` and every channel/connection loss with the delivery unacked (measured below).
- `delivery-limit: -1` (unlimited) on every queue no stage consumes. RabbitMQ 4.x gives a quorum queue without a policy limit a default of 20 and drops a message over it when the queue has no dead-letter target; replay scans and operator inspection return messages with `nack(requeue)`, which counts. Measured on 4.1.8: without `-1` a message was gone at the 22nd return; with `-1` it was still there after 30 (step 3.2 review round 1).
- `reject-publish` on a work queue nacks the relay's publish; the outbox row stays unpublished with backoff (backpressure into MySQL, nothing lost). A nack is infrastructure: it never counts as an attempt, so a queue that stays full never turns rows into poison. Work queues never drop.

### The lossy work queue (v5)

`scan.url.inspect` is consumed (it is a work queue: consumer, retries, dead letters, replay), but it is a **bounded lossy buffer by design** (step 4.3 U2, U15):

- The browser worker is an **optional** deployable (Compose profile `url-browser`; production may not run it). Without a consumer a work-policy queue would grow to a million messages and then `reject-publish` would nack the relay's publishes of `inspection_requested.v1`, leaving outbox rows unpublished with backoff: dynamic inspection would slow the outbox, which must never happen. With `drop-head` at 10 000 the oldest requests are dropped instead.
- A dropped request is not lost silently: its `url_dynamic_inspections` row stays `pending` and the email-worker's sweeper marks it `expired` after `URL_DYNAMIC_PENDING_TTL_SECONDS` (`scan_url_dynamic_inspections_total{status="expired"}`, alert `UrlDynamicInspectionsExpiring`). The round never waits for an inspection; its results act at click time only.
- RabbitMQ dead-letters quorum-queue messages **at least once only with `overflow reject-publish`**; with `drop-head` the strategy falls back to at-most-once, so the policy states `at-most-once`. Heads dropped at `max-length` are dead-lettered (reason `maxlen`, key `dead-letter.scan.url.inspect`) at most once into `email.dead-letter` (itself bounded, `reject-publish`); `delivery-limit` and the consumer's own dead letters work as on every work queue.

### The dormant-queue rule

Queues of stages that do not exist yet are declared and bound now (the brief's fixed list), with **no consumer** (no mock consumers). Without a bound they would grow by up to a million messages a day each, and with `reject-publish` they would block every publish of `mail.message.parsed.v1` to the working queues. So they are **bounded lossy buffers** (`max-length 10000`, `drop-head`) and **never the source of truth**. When its stage is built, that stage's step:

1. switches the queue to the work policy (a policy change in `TOPOLOGY`, no re-declare),
2. adds a role user (or extends one) with read on the queue in `init.mjs`, a ledger consumer name and a `RECONCILE_QUEUES` entry,
3. onboards older messages from MySQL (the outbox and the stage's tables), not from the queue.

The `DormantQueueAtLimit` alert (WP3) shows when a dormant queue is full and dropping.

## Dead-letter paths and retry hops

Behaviour of the consumer library (Q8, **step 3.2 WP2**):

- **Hold** (`email.received`, the `scan.*` work queues, dormant queues): on a retryable or transient failure the consumer keeps the delivery (its prefetch slot) for the backoff delay (≤ 60 s), republishes a copy with `retry.<queue>.org.<id>.<type>` to the home exchange, waits for the confirm and acks the original.
- **Delay queue** (`email.delivery.pending`): the consumer republishes a copy to `email.delivery` with `delay.email.delivery.pending.org.<id>.<type>` and a per-message `expiration`, confirms, acks. `email.delivery.retry` has no consumer; on expiry the broker dead-letters the copy with `retry.email.delivery.pending.expired` back to `email.delivery.pending`. Quorum queues expire at the head only, so a short delay behind a longer one waits for it (lateness ≤ `maxDelayMs`).
- **Dead letter**: after `maxAttempts` counted failures, `maxRetryAgeMs` of transient retries, a fatal error or a contract failure, the consumer republishes a copy with `dead-letter.<queue>.org.<id>.<type>` and the failure headers to `email.events`. Crash loops hit `delivery-limit` and the broker dead-letters with `dead-letter.<queue>`. Both land in `email.dead-letter`.
- Backoff: `d = min(maxDelayMs, baseDelayMs · 2^(n−1))`, delay ∈ `[d/2, d]` (equal jitter). A copy keeps `messageId = eventId` and `correlationId`, so ledgers absorb duplicates.
- **Replay** (`POST /api/v1/platform/dead-letters/replay`, `platform:dead_letter_replay` + MFA, audited; **step 3.2 WP4**) republishes dead letters of a work queue with `retry.<sourceQueue>.org.<id>.<type>` and fresh retry headers.

The delay → expiry → work-queue path and the internal keys were checked against the local broker during WP1 (a throwaway vhost: an event reached `email.received` and `email.analytics`, a retry key only `email.received`, a broker-style `dead-letter.email.received` only `email.dead-letter`, and a 1.5 s delay hop returned to `email.delivery.pending`).

Recovery and retry behaviour (contract, verified by the unit and integration suites):

- **Connection.** Connects in the background (module init never awaits the broker). On an unexpected `error`/`close` the connection is marked down at once, amqplib closes every channel (pending confirms reject), and the client reconnects with equal-jitter backoff (default 500 ms – 30 s; a connection that lived < 5 s counts as a failed attempt) rotating through the URLs, then re-runs every `onConnected` setup in registration order. It **never exits the process**. `checkHealth` = connected, not `blocked`, every object of `requireTopology` verified passively once per connection (a missing exchange/queue keeps readiness red and is re-checked on the next probe).
- **Publisher.** One confirm channel, created lazily and re-created after a broker close with a short backoff (100 ms – 5 s). Every path runs the content guard on the envelope (or the raw bytes for `republish`) and its headers, plus the size check, before sending (`refused` + code, nothing sent). Per item: broker ack → `confirmed`; a `basic.return` before the ack → `returned` (`code: NO_ROUTE`); broker nack → `nacked`; no confirm within `confirmTimeoutMs` → `timeout`, and the channel is closed and re-created (every unresolved item of that channel becomes `timeout`). Channel or connection death with confirms pending rejects the call with `BrokerUnavailableError` (`code` = the AMQP reply name, e.g. `ACCESS_REFUSED`). While the connection is `blocked`, a batch waits for `unblocked` up to `confirmTimeoutMs`, then returns `timeout` for all items.
- **Consumer.** One channel per consumer, `prefetch` = max deliveries in flight, `noAck: false`. A channel closed by the broker (or a broker `basic.cancel`) re-subscribes after backoff (1 s – 30 s) on the same connection; a lost connection re-subscribes through `onConnected`. A handler result for a dead channel is dropped (debug log); the broker redelivers. Decision table: `handled`/`duplicate` → ack; decode failure → dead-letter at once (`contract_invalid`, `unknown_event_type`, `unsupported_schema_version`, `tenant_mismatch`); `NonRetryableError` → dead-letter with its code (`handler_rejected` if not a `[a-z0-9_]{1,48}` token); transient → `x-eg-transient-retries + 1` (no attempt), dead-letter `retry_age_exceeded` past `maxRetryAgeMs`; anything else → `x-eg-attempt + 1`, dead-letter `max_attempts_exceeded` when it reaches `maxAttempts`. `x-eg-attempts` on a dead letter = counted failures including a fatal one. Copies drop the broker headers (`x-death`, `x-first-death-*`, `x-last-death-*`, `x-delivery-count`) and the previous failure headers.
- **Never acked without a confirmed copy.** Hold: wait the delay holding the slot, republish, confirm, ack; an unconfirmed copy → `nack(requeue)`. Delay queue: republish with `expiration`, confirm, ack; unconfirmed → hold up to `min(delay, 60 s)` then `nack(requeue)`. Dead letter: unconfirmed → hold one base backoff then `nack(requeue)`. If the content guard refuses the dead-letter copy (the delivery itself carries content), the original is rejected (`nack`, no requeue) to the queue policy's broker dead-letter target (`dead-letter.<queue>` for work queues; dormant queues have none and drop it) — the library never copies content.
- **stop()**: cancel the subscription, release held deliveries (`nack(requeue)`), wait for running handlers up to `stopTimeoutMs` (default 30 s), close the channel (the broker requeues anything left).
- **Logs** carry tokens only: per delivery a logger child `{ queue, eventId, eventType, organizationId, correlationId }`, plus `errName`, `errCode`, `failureClass`, `attempt`, `reason`, AMQP reply names and node indexes. Never `err.message`, error objects, payload values, broker reply text or URLs (they carry credentials).

Consequences for the library and its users:

- Every `nack(requeue)` the library issues (unconfirmed retry/dead-letter copy, `stop()` during a hold) and every crash or connection loss spends one of the 20 returns of `delivery-limit`. A message whose copies cannot be confirmed 20 times in a row (e.g. `email.dead-letter` full under `reject-publish`) is dead-lettered by the broker with `delivery_limit` instead of looping forever. Application retries (hold / delay copies) are new messages and reset the count, so retries bounded by `maxAttempts` / `maxRetryAgeMs` never hit the limit.
- Broker dead letters carry no `x-eg-failure-*` headers; replay (Q9) reads `x-first-death-queue` / `x-death` for them.

### Prefetch and the unacked counters

`messaging-client.test.ts` ("prefetch backpressure", step 4.4 §9, after the 4.3 §13.10 sample of 9 > 8) asserts three counters of different strength:

1. **Client in-flight (hard bound).** Deliveries handed to the handler and not yet acked or nacked on the channel, counted by the test (incremented at delivery, decremented after `channel.ack`/`nack`): `≤ prefetch` at every delivery, and the handler concurrency reaches exactly `prefetch`. This holds by construction of AMQP `basic.qos`: the broker never has more than `prefetch` unacknowledged deliveries outstanding on the consumer's channel, and the client can only hold deliveries it was sent.
2. **The consumer's channel (hard bound).** The management API's `messages_unacknowledged` of **this consumer's channel**, found through the queue's `consumer_details` by the consumer tag (never the sum over all channels of the vhost, which could include a leftover consumer of another test): `≤ prefetch` in every sample. The channel process is the one that enforces `basic.qos`, so its counter is consistent with the bound.
3. **The quorum queue's counter (statistical bound).** The queue's own `messages_unacknowledged` is maintained by the quorum queue's Ra state machine, which applies a client's settle only when its command is applied to the log, while the channel has already re-credited the consumer and the next deliveries are checked out; the management statistics are also sampled per process at different moments. The excess is therefore the number of settles not yet applied — it grows with the load on the broker's host, so **no constant multiple of the prefetch bounds every sample or even the 95th percentile**: seen 5 on a quiet broker, and p95 = 9 (> 2 × 4) during a whole-chain run (step 4.4 §12 WP5 deviation 5). The suite records the distribution and asserts only **median ≤ 2 × prefetch** and **every sample ≤ the backlog (200)** — a regression that ignored prefetch would push the median far above 2 × prefetch, while settles in flight cannot. Backpressure is proven by (1) and (2).

The run's line `[messaging-client] prefetch=… client_in_flight_max=… channel_unacked_max=… queue_unacked_p50=… queue_unacked_p95=… queue_unacked_max=…` records the measured values (written before the assertions).
