### 1.2 Headline gaps

1. **No sandbox execution, and the planned path is the wrong one.** Production may run `disabled` or `static` only; the 4.6 `vm` backend is a preflight that always refuses, and it targets an in-house Linux VM runtime in Kubernetes that cannot analyse Windows or Office behaviour and that the brief rules out without a separate security review (B7.6–B7.14, B7.24; §3.1).
2. **No threat-intelligence feed platform.** Only analyst-entered platform indicators and hashes exist: no feeds, sources, schedules, sync logs, tenant indicators or IOC store, and confidence is stored but ignored, so a future unverified feed entry would hard-block mail (B11.x, B5.3, B5.4; §3.3).
3. **No QR-code or image-based phishing protection.** Nothing in the repository decodes images (B9.1–B9.9, B3.11; §3.5).
4. **No campaigns and no incident handling.** No cross-message correlation, no incident object or SOC escalation, and late detections raise no events or alerts (B12.x, B15.9, B16.16, B17.9–B17.11; §3.6).
5. **Deep static analysis sits outside the decision.** PDF, Office, HTML and script analyzers exist but run only in the asynchronous, opt-in sandbox, whose result never changes the round; late results (sandbox revision 1) never re-evaluate policy (B6.3, B6.4, B6.16, B8.1–B8.6, B14.6, B19.13).
6. Smaller gaps: the 0–100 ATP score and bands (B14.1, B14.12), confidence (B8.19, B14.17), URL depth (suspicious TLDs, path and query, downloads, URLs in PDF/Office), AI analyst features and an authorization record (B13.x), the ATP portal pages (sandbox, TI feeds, campaigns, incidents, URL search), false-positive monitoring (B19.11).

### 1.3 What exists and must be reused, not duplicated

- **Scan pipeline** (ADR-0026): rounds, engine registry, `scan_engine_results`, typed child rows written in the finalize transaction, stage-2 triggers, deadline sweeper. QR decoding, deep static analysis and campaign fingerprints plug into it.
- **URL engine, url-security, click-service, isolated browser** (4.3): extraction, normalization, lookup providers with an HMAC Redis cache, lookalikes, the SSRF-guarded fetcher and egress proxy, `url-map/1`, rewriting, click tokens and the time-of-click recheck. QR and threat intelligence extend them; no second URL engine.
- **Attachment engine** (4.2): ClamAV, YARA-X, the resource-limited inspector pool, archive walker, known-hash catalog, content cache, signature rescans.
- **BEC engine** (4.4): 50 explainable rules, executive and vendor profiles, trusted senders, opt-in history, and the AI layer with its invariants and Anthropic provider (fake provider in tests).
- **Sandbox orchestration** (4.6): the credential-poor controller, the `SandboxBackend` contract, static analyzers, sealed samples and artifacts, result MAC, deduplication, revision-1 risk. A supported sandbox is one more backend.
- **Risk scoring** (5.1): the ATP score is a view of `risk-score/1` with new signals, not a second score.
- **Policy DSL, policy engine, quarantine** (5.2, 5.3) with dry-runs and the 7.3 builder: ATP adds fields and one action (`ESCALATE`).
- **Threat-intelligence tables and APIs** (4.2, 4.3, 6.1), **messaging** (3.2: outbox, relay, quorum queues, retries, dead letters, ledger, content guard), **webhooks and alerts** (6.2), **admin portal** (7.1–7.3: design system, BFF, explorer, details, settings editors).

## 3. Notes that need care

### 3.1 Sandbox

**What step 4.6 built** (`S4.6`, ADR-0039, ADR-0040, `docs/security/sandbox-threat-model.md`):

- An isolation contract, `SandboxBackend` (`sbx/backends/backend.ts`): `selfTest`, `supports`, `run`, `reap`, `close`, with isolation classes `none`, `process`, `container` (explicitly not a VM boundary) and `vm`. The `sandbox-controller` holds no database, Redis, mail-bucket, token, DLP, DKIM or AI credential (configuration refuses those keys by name), only its broker user, a sandbox-bucket user (`samples/` read, `artifacts/` write), the sample and result keys and the artifact public key.
- Backends: `disabled` (default everywhere); `static` (production-eligible, no execution): pure TypeScript analyzers for PE, ELF, OLE/VBA, OOXML, PDF, HTML/SVG, scripts, LNK and generic checks (entropy, EICAR, embedded hosts) in a forked child with Node's permission model, an empty environment and RSS/CPU/time kills (`sbx/backends/static/*`); `hardened-container`: a Docker runner for local/CI tests only (strace, sinkhole), refused in production and in Kubernetes (`sbx/runner/*`, `sbx/backends/factory.ts`); `vm`: a preflight that detects Kubernetes, reads the RuntimeClass through a minimal in-cluster client (`sbx/backends/vm/k8s-client.ts`), checks the handler against an allow-list, and then always refuses with `vm_runner_not_implemented` (`sbx/backends/vm/vm.backend.ts`).
- The engine side (`ew/sandbox/*`): asynchronous and optional (plan feature `attachment_sandbox` plus tenant opt-in, default off), only `suspicious`/`unknown` attachments (at most 5 per message), content-hash deduplication across tenants, sealed samples deleted after the result, one-time result tokens with a MAC over the whole result, `sandbox-map/1` (no `clean` without VM isolation), and a revision-1 risk score. The round never waits and the policy decision is never revisited.

**Why execution is disabled.** No VM-class runner exists, container isolation shares the host kernel, and none of the nine gates of threat model §8 is met (VM isolation proven at startup and continuously, out-of-guest collection, a dedicated tainted node pool without useful credentials, default-deny networking to a sinkhole only, a constrained controller identity, the escape suite plus an external penetration test, alert routing and a suspected-escape runbook, a patch cadence, a re-reviewed threat model with an accepting ADR). VM isolation cannot be verified on this workstation (`S4.6 §13.6`).

**Why the planned `vm` path does not meet the brief.** It targets gVisor, Kata or Firecracker inside Kubernetes, which run Linux workloads. Windows executables, Office macros and registry behaviour need Windows guests with Office installed, and the brief says to use an existing supported sandbox and not to build an execution environment from scratch without a separate security architecture review. A CAPE-style deployment (KVM/QEMU on x86-64 hosts, disposable Windows guests reverted to a clean snapshot after every task, an isolated detonation network with simulated services) or a commercial product (appliance or cloud API) fits that rule; an in-house runner does not.

**What any VM-isolated runner needs:** x86-64 hosts with hardware virtualization (bare metal or instances that expose it); golden guest images for the Windows and Office versions in use, with licences; snapshot revert per task; monitoring the sample cannot tamper with; a detonation network with no route to production networks, cloud metadata or internal DNS, simulated Internet by default and any real egress only through a separately reviewed, attributable path; credentials scoped to the sandbox only; capacity sized to the submission rate (only `suspicious`/`unknown` attachments, deduplicated by hash); patching, monitoring and an escape runbook.

**Recommendation.** Add one backend (working name `external`, with a product-specific client such as CAPE's REST API) behind the existing `SandboxBackend` interface in the existing `sandbox-controller`. Everything upstream stays: dispatch, deduplication, sealed samples, result MAC, the `scan.sandbox*` queues, the finalizer and revision 1. The adapter submits the sample it already opens, polls with bounded backoff inside the analysis deadline (detonation needs a longer task timeout and deadline than today's 180 s and 900 s), treats the report as untrusted input (size cap, strict schema, bounded mapping into `SandboxObservations` plus a report view v2 with signatures, family, registry and mutex facts, ATT&CK technique ids and network indicators), fetches screenshots within the artifact caps, and deletes the task at the sandbox afterwards. It reports `isolation = vm` only for a product configured as VM-based whose health check passes, and records product and version in `backend_version`. `sandbox-map/2` adds family and signature rules and keeps rule C1 (clean only after a complete VM run of minimum duration without findings or evasion). Configuration gains only the endpoint and its credential (mTLS or API key) as allowed keys, with an egress policy to that endpoint only.

**Tests.** Contract tests against a local fake sandbox service: a test-only Compose service like `infra/url-test` and `infra/sandbox/sinkhole` (a script mounted read-only) that serves scripted responses: malicious, benign, evasive, timeout, slow, HTTP 5xx, malformed, oversized, wrong task id, replayed. Nothing is executed and no real malware is used (synthetic files and EICAR built at test time). The local escape-resistance suite does not apply to an external product; its equivalent is the vendor's hardening plus a penetration test in staging.

**On this developer machine** (Apple Silicon Mac, Docker Desktop with a 16 GB VM): verifiable are the adapter logic, mapping, timeouts, retries, cleanup calls, the MAC flow, end-to-end stack runs against the fake, and the portal pages. Not verifiable: real detonation, Windows guests (no x86 hardware virtualization and no nested KVM in Docker Desktop; emulation would not be representative), isolation and escape resistance, evasion handling, sandbox throughput, the real hosts' network isolation, and Kubernetes NetworkPolicies (rendered only). ATP-6 records these as not verified.

**Items for the separate security architecture review** (a gate before any execution, §5 D1): the product and who operates it; where samples go (tenant files leaving the platform for a third-party cloud need contractual and data-residency approval); the detonation network and Internet policy (abuse from our addresses); host hardening and identity (no cloud credentials on hosts, metadata blocked); controller-to-sandbox authentication and allow-listed egress; parsing of untrusted reports; retention and deletion of samples and reports at the sandbox; artifact key custody (no service holds the artifact private key today, so an artifact viewer needs a design); monitoring, patch cadence and incident response; an updated threat model and an ADR that supersedes ADR-0039's `vm` path; a staging penetration test.

### 3.2 AI-assisted analysis

**What exists** (`S4.4 B9/B10`, ADR-0034): providers behind `BecAiProvider` (`bec/ai/types.ts`): the local logistic model `bec-lr/1` (default `shadow`) and Anthropic Claude through the official SDK (`bec/ai/anthropic.ts`; default `off`, platform cap `BEC_LLM_MODE`, tenant opt-in `ai.llm`). The request carries a feature vector, categorical identity facts and a redacted excerpt of at most 1 500 characters (addresses, URLs, numbers, phones, profile names and tenant labels replaced, `bec/text/excerpt.ts`); the output is a strict JSON schema of enums, scores and spans with no free text; per-tenant daily and platform per-minute budgets in Redis (never called without a budget check), a breaker and timeouts; the raw response is never stored or logged, only its hash. Aggregation invariants: the AI never produces `clean`, never lowers a verdict, raises to `suspicious` at most on its own and contributes to `malicious` only with deterministic identity and intent evidence within a band. Risk scoring uses deterministic codes only, and the DSL refuses AI-derived fields in hold rules. Tests use `BEC_LLM_PROVIDER=fake` (refused in production) and an injected transport; no real LLM call has been made (`S4.4 §13.6`).

**Prompt injection today** is bounded structurally: the template tells the model the excerpt is untrusted, the model can only pick enum labels and indicators and excerpt spans that are validated, so an injected instruction can at most suppress or add a bounded `suspicious` raise. No adversarial corpus exists (B13.9).

**What §13 adds and how.** Explanations, summaries and remediation advice are free text, so a mail or document can steer the narrative even though it cannot steer the verdict. Proposed rules for ATP-8:

- On demand only, triggered by an analyst (never in the mail path), behind its own tenant authorization per feature and its own permission. Deterministic template summaries are the default when AI is off.
- Input is structured facts (codes, findings, evidence ids, counts) by default; message text only as the existing redacted excerpt and only when the tenant's authorization covers it.
- Output is bounded text with mandatory citations of evidence ids that the server validates, labelled as AI-generated and advisory, and stored without message content (or not stored, §5 D3). Recommendations name manual actions the analyst may take (release, rescan, add an indicator); nothing is executed.
- An authorization record per tenant (who, when, basis, provider, region, data categories, provider-side retention), MFA, audited and revocable, following the DLP Aadhaar compliance record (ADR-0038).
- Reuse: the provider, budget, breaker and structured-output code moves from `packages/bec/src/ai` into a shared package with no behaviour change for BEC; no second LLM client.
- Tests: a prompt-injection corpus (synthetic mails and documents that address the model) against the fake provider and recorded fixtures, citation validation, marker strings that must never be sent; no test calls a real LLM.

### 3.3 Threat intelligence

**What exists:** `threat_indicators` (platform analyst rows only; tenant scope modelled through the generated `org_scope` but unused; types `ip`, `domain`, `url`, `email`, `file_sha256`; severity, confidence, category, source, expiry), `malware_signatures` (hash catalog; sources `analyst`, `sandbox`, `feed:<name>`), and four reserved, unused tables (`ip_reputation`, `domain_reputation`, `url_reputation`, `yara_rules`). The api offers platform CRUD (`/platform/threat-indicators`, `/platform/threat-hashes`; MFA, audited) and the audited tenant lookup `GET /threat-intelligence/lookup` (`api/threat-intelligence/*`). Lookup-time providers `local_feed` (uncached), `dnsbl` and `rdap` sit behind breakers and an HMAC-authenticated Redis cache (`url-security/reputation/*`). Rspamd uses RBL and URL lists by configuration (`infra/rspamd/entrypoint.sh`, test profile locally). Portal page 15 shows lookup and platform management.

**Design for ATP-1:**

- Two adapter kinds: lookup-time (the existing `ReputationProvider`, for DNS lists and real-time APIs) and feed ingestion (new `FeedProvider`: periodic download, normalization through url-security, upsert). Formats as adapters: plain lists, CSV, JSON APIs, STIX 2.1 over TAXII 2.1, MISP feeds. The first adapters follow §5 D2.
- Ingestion outside the mail path in an isolated workload: proposed is a new `intel-worker` deployable in this monorepo (not a separate application), with a database account limited to the TI tables, no S3, egress only through the SSRF-guarded fetcher and the egress proxy, source credentials encrypted like webhook secrets (ADR-0055), leader-locked schedules, diff-based upserts, expiry, sync-run records and metrics. Running it inside the internet-facing api is rejected for the same reason the webhook-worker exists.
- Hot path: MySQL stays authoritative; a Redis cache keyed by type and value hash, invalidated by a global generation number that every sync or analyst change increments, plus short negative caching. An analyst change still applies to the next scan and click.
- Mapping: source trust and indicator confidence decide whether a hit is `malicious` (rule M1, hard block), `suspicious` or informational. Analyst rows keep today's behaviour; unverified open-source data never causes a hard block on its own (B11.24).
- Tenant indicators affect only their tenant's verdicts and never the shared caches (cache keys of tenant rows include the organization), following the reasoning of `S4.2 A17` and `S4.3 §7` against cross-tenant influence.
- Sightings (`last_seen_at`, hit counts) are aggregated in Redis and flushed, never written per message.
- Licences and keys are operator-configured and nothing ships enabled. Tests never reach the public Internet: a local fixture feed server (test profile, `.test` names and documentation IP ranges, like `infra/url-test`).
- Optional export of high-confidence indicators to Rspamd maps for SMTP-time use; risk scoring already de-duplicates Rspamd and URL-engine evidence (`url_threat` is a `max` group).
- ATO 8.4 consumes the same lookup for IP reputation instead of a separate `ato_ip_reputation_cache`.

### 3.4 Time-of-click

**Done in 4.3** (B5.16–B5.28): a separate public `click-service` with its own database account (`gateway_click`: SELECT on six tables, INSERT on two), rate limits, deny-all CSP pages, no open redirect, and a recheck within 2.5 s: the latest stored result, the latest dynamic inspection, fresh reputation (local feed uncached), the host's addresses, the redirect chain re-resolved through the SSRF guard and lookalikes of a changed final host; the tenant's click policy; one event row and one outbox event per click; database down → 503.

**Gaps against brief §5:**

1. Re-evaluation sees only platform indicators without confidence (fixed by ATP-1).
2. Links that are never rewritten bypass time-of-click: links in attachments, in signed or encrypted mail (skipped by design) and in QR codes (a phone scan never reaches the click-service unless the image is re-rendered, §5 D8).
3. One rewritten copy serves every recipient, so clicks are not attributable to users (`S4.3 §10`); ATO and incident work would benefit from per-recipient tokens (a delivery-path change, not proposed in ATP by default).
4. `allowContinue` defaults to true (a tenant choice); no other gap in the warn and block pages.
5. SSRF and DNS rebinding protections are implemented and tested; the production egress-proxy mode and a production recursive resolver were not run end to end locally.
6. Safe browsing: dynamic inspection covers at most three scan-time URLs per message; a URL that was clean at scan time and changed later gets the static recheck only. A synchronous browser run does not fit the 2.5 s budget; an asynchronous "warn and inspect" mode is possible.
7. A link that now serves a file is not scanned at click time (B5.13).
8. High availability (three replicas, PDB) is rendered, not measured.

### 3.5 QR codes

- **Placement:** inside the existing `url` engine (stage 1) as a new extraction source, not a new engine. Decoding yields URLs, and every later step already exists (normalization, reputation, lookalikes, fetch, verdict, rows, BEC rules K1–K4, risk). A separate engine would duplicate URL logic or need a stage-2 dependency that breaks the depth-1 rule of `S4.4 B2`.
- **Inputs:** image parts (inline `cid:` images and attached images are both `email_attachments` rows) of type PNG, JPEG, GIF (first frame), BMP and WebP; `data:` images in the HTML body (ignored by today's extractor). Later, after measuring cost: images embedded in PDF and Office files. Not proposed: rasterising SVG (script and entity risk) and codes drawn with HTML tables.
- **Library choice** (a spike in ATP-2; exact versions pinned and at least 14 days old, as for every dependency here): `zxing-wasm` (ZXing C++ compiled to WebAssembly; QR, Micro QR, rMQR and other symbologies; architecture-independent, so it runs on the arm64 development machine and x64 production), `@zxing/library` (TypeScript port in maintenance mode) or `jsQR` (QR only, little recent maintenance); the spike confirms maintenance state, licences and decode rates on generated test images before a choice. Image decoding by pure-JavaScript decoders with resolution and memory limits, or by `sharp` (libvips: fast and broad, but native with a larger attack surface). Recommended: a WebAssembly symbol decoder and image decoding inside the existing resource-limited child-process pool pattern (`ew/engines/attachment/inspector/pool.ts`, also used by the DLP extractor), so a decoder crash or hang kills a child, never the worker.
- **Resource limits** (validated configuration): bytes per image, pixels per image (checked from the header before decoding), images per message, frames per image, CPU and wall time per image and per message, total decoded bytes per message, a small fixed set of scales and rotations. A limit hit is `qr.decode_incomplete`, never clean (as rule E1 of `S4.3 U4`).
- **Output:** payloads classified (URL, text, Wi-Fi, tel/sms, contact); URLs normalized and stored in `message_urls` with source `qr` (never rewritten) and selected for scan-time fetching, because no click-time check exists for a phone scan; per-image findings in a new partitioned child table (part index, symbol type, payload kind, `url_seq`, decode status; other payloads only as hashes).
- **Verdict association:** an informational `url.from_qr` code; QR URLs use the existing rules (for example M3 for a credential form behind a lookalike); risk signals join the existing URL groups so nothing is counted twice, with a `qr_present` signal that adds weight only together with another finding; DSL fields `urls[].source` and `qr.count`.
- **Optional** (§5 D8): re-render QR codes to point at the click-service; this changes images in delivered mail and is not proposed by default.

### 3.6 Campaign correlation

- **Fingerprints** (no content stored): URL identity hash, final host and registrable domains, attachment SHA-256 (all exist); a template fingerprint (SimHash over shingles of normalized subject and body tokens with addresses, numbers and names masked, computed where the text is already read, the `bec` engine, and stored as 64-bit values); sending infrastructure (client network /24 or /48, DKIM `d=`, envelope, From and Reply-To registrable domains); the impersonated target (executive, vendor or brand id, lookalike target domain).
- **Tenant level** (default): a correlator consumes `mail.message.scanned.v1` (later also `sandbox_completed.v1` and policy decisions), keeps short-lived windows in Redis (for example 72 hours, keys per organization) and persists a campaign once enough related messages with at least one threat signal exist; idempotent per event (ledger), bounded fan-out, deterministic campaign ids per organization and seed indicator.
- **Global level** (only where policy permits, §5 D4): only on indicators that are already platform facts (malicious URL identity hashes, malware hashes, infrastructure of confirmed detections); never template fingerprints of clean mail, subjects, addresses or tenant identities. A tenant sees at most a bucketed "also seen by other organizations" count for indicators in its own mail; a per-organization contract flag and opt-out; a minimum number of organizations (for example three) before a global campaign exists.
- **Persistence:** `threat_campaigns` (tenant rows; platform rows with `organization_id` NULL readable only through the tenant's own membership), `campaign_members` (partitioned by `received_at`), `campaign_indicators` (links to TI); events `mail.campaign.detected.v1` and `.updated.v1` (metadata only) for webhooks, alerts and incidents.
- **Effects:** a campaign can only make later decisions stricter (next rounds, click time, retro alerts). Mail already delivered needs post-delivery remediation (8.2) or an analyst.
- **Limits:** long-range hunting needs the planned search and analytics stores (OpenSearch, ClickHouse; ADR-0009, no plan step yet). ATP-5 stays within bounded MySQL windows and says so.

### 3.7 Unified ATP score versus the 5.1 risk score

- No second score and no `atp_risk_*` table: `message_risk_scores` (deterministic, versioned, explainable, de-duplicated, tenant profiles, dry-run) is the unified score.
- **ATP view:** `atpScore = floor(score / 10)` (0–100) and `atpSeverity` from the level (`none` and `low` → LOW, `medium` → MEDIUM, `high` → HIGH, `critical` → CRITICAL) under the tenant's effective thresholds; exposed in the scans and messages APIs, the DSL (`atp.severity`), explorer filters and reports.
- **Bands:** the brief's initial bands equal thresholds medium 300, high 500, critical 750 on the internal scale, which today's tenant bounds already allow; the platform default is 300/550/800. Either tenants adopt the brief's bands through a profile, or the platform ships `rsk-default/2` with them after re-running the synthetic calibration (`docs/performance/risk-scoring-calibration.md`) (§5 D6).
- **New signals** (`risk-signals/2`): QR (inside the URL groups), deep static attachment codes (`attachment_risky`), TI with confidence and source trust (`url_threat`, `reputation_*`, a new sender-TI signal), sender-domain age, campaign membership (later revisions only), ATO account risk (after 8.4). A confidence per contribution (exact deterministic match high, heuristics medium, feed hits from source trust and indicator confidence; AI excluded).
- **Late inputs:** today there are revision 0 (the round) and revision 1 (the sandbox), and the policy engine never re-evaluates (`S5.2 P13`, §10). ATP-4 decides by ADR; recommended: revisions only for inputs that belong to the round, plus a retro-evaluation path that only escalates (alert, incident, quarantine when not yet delivered, clawback through 8.2).

### 3.11 Compatibility with ATO (8.4/8.5) and journaling/archiving (8.1)

**ATO:**

- **Incidents:** one shared `security_incidents` model (with a kind such as `atp` or `ato`, links to messages, campaigns and accounts, assignment, states, notes, audit). The ATO brief forbids duplicate incident management, and its proposed `ato_incidents`/`ato_incident_events` would otherwise duplicate ATP's. Whichever of 8.4 and ATP-4 starts first builds it; the other reuses it (§5 D15).
- **Threat intelligence:** 8.4 uses ATP-1's lookup, feeds and Redis cache for IP reputation instead of `ato_ip_reputation_cache`.
- **Outbound findings:** 8.4 needs BEC and phishing findings on outbound mail; the `bec` engine skips outbound today (`S4.4 B1`). One outbound mode serves both; the owner (ATP-7 or 8.4) is decided with the 8.4 design.
- **Account risk as input:** brief §4 "integrate with ATO signals" needs 8.4's account risk score as an input of risk scoring and a BEC rule (`risk-input/2`).
- **Responses:** sending restrictions and identity-provider actions stay ATO's; ATP's `ESCALATE` links an ATO incident when the sender is an internal account. 8.2 connectors and 8.5 provider adapters are also the route for post-delivery remediation of retroactive ATP verdicts.
- **Click attribution:** per-recipient tokens would give ATO "user clicked a phishing link" signals; today one token serves every recipient.

**Journaling and archiving (8.1):**

- 8.1 links journal records to the existing rounds and `scan_engine_results` without rescanning. ATP stores its per-round results (QR findings, deep static codes, TI matches) in round-keyed tables, so those links cover them without 8.1 changes.
- Late results (sandbox revision 1, campaign membership, retro TI hits, incidents) arrive after journaling; 8.1 should accept "security result linked" updates (its brief lists `journal.security.scan.linked`), and ATP emits events for them.
- Retention differs: per-round rows live 6 months, archives years. 8.1 should snapshot a bounded scan summary (verdicts, codes, versions, ATP score and severity, campaign ids) at capture time; ATP extends that summary additively.
- Legal hold does not keep sandbox samples (deleted after analysis); artifacts follow their own retention unless §5 D5 decides otherwise. Journal exports exclude sandbox artifacts and licence-restricted TI source data.
- Journaling never triggers rescans; ATP rescans create new rounds that 8.1 links additively.

## 4. Proposed implementation steps

### 4.1 Rules for every ATP step

- The execution-plan loop applies (inspect, design note and ADRs by Agent 1, implement, verify, adversarial review, commit). Work packages inside a step run in parallel only on disjoint files; anything touching `schema.prisma`, `packages/*` contracts, the topology or `app.module.ts` is sequenced.

### 4.12 Recommended order and placement

1. **ATP-1** first: every later step reads threat intelligence, and 8.4 can reuse its lookup instead of building IP reputation.
2. **ATP-2** then **ATP-3**: both extend the URL and attachment engines and must be sequenced; QR first because it is the larger product gap and smaller change.
3. **ATP-4** next, after the incident model is agreed with 8.4 (whichever of the two starts first builds it).
4. **ATP-5** and **ATP-6**: ATP-6's WP1 (the security review, documentation only) can start as soon as §5 D1 is decided, in parallel with earlier steps; its implementation follows ATP-4.
5. **ATP-7** after 8.4 (its WP4 needs ATO); WP1–WP3 could move earlier if 8.4 is late.
6. **ATP-8**, then **ATP-9**.

Relative to the plan: 8.1 (journaling) can run before or after ATP-1–ATP-3 because ATP stores per-round results in round-keyed tables (§3.11); if 8.1 runs first, its scan summary must be designed for additive ATP fields. Post-delivery remediation of retroactive verdicts (ATP-4, ATP-5, ATP-6) depends on 8.2; until then ATP raises alerts and incidents only.

## 6. Risks and honest limitations

- **No zero-day guarantee.** Static analysis, heuristics, threat intelligence and sandboxing reduce risk; none guarantees detection of unseen threats. Sandboxes are evaded (environment checks, delays, user-interaction triggers); `sandbox-map` treats evasion as `unknown`, never clean.
- **Threat intelligence is not proof.** Feeds contain false positives, stale entries and poisoned data. Unverified indicators must not cause hard blocks alone (B11.24); every block from a feed must name its source.
- **Accuracy is unmeasured.** All corpora are synthetic; no detection rate or false-positive rate on real mail is known (5.1 says so). ATP-9 adds false-positive measurement, not validated accuracy.
- **Sandbox execution cannot be verified here**, and it adds operational risk (escape, abuse from our addresses, licences, capacity). Until ATP-6 passes the review and staging tests, production stays `disabled` or `static`.
- **Performance at 1M mails/day is not verified.** QR decoding, synchronous static analysis and correlation add CPU on the mail path; each step must benchmark on the laptop and state that production numbers need 9.1–9.2.
- **Privacy:** global correlation, third-party sandboxes and external AI move tenant-derived data across boundaries; each needs a decision (§5) and a record.
- **Prompt injection is bounded, not eliminated.** Free-text AI summaries can still be misleading; they stay advisory, cited and labelled.
- **Time-of-click has blind spots:** QR codes, attachment links and signed mail are not rewritten; clicks are not attributable to users.
- **Retroactive verdicts cannot pull delivered mail** until the 8.2 connectors exist; ATP alerts and opens incidents instead.
- **Known platform residuals carry over:** the Prisma wrong-read defect is mitigated by echo checks, not removed, so every new read that decides something must be echo-checked; Kubernetes manifests are rendered only; CI has not run.
